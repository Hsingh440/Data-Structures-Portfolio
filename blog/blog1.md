What is data science to me?
Starting this course, I see data science as much more than writing code or building models. It is a way to understand the world, identify patterns, and make informed decisions. Since data increasingly comes from people and communities, learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts is important because it reminds us that behind every dataset are real individuals with unique experiences. Numbers can tell us a lot, but they never capture the full story without considering the human perspective.

One reason I think it is important to model social topics and people is that it can help solve real-world problems. For example, data science can be used to improve healthcare, education, transportation, and public policy. By analyzing trends, researchers and organizations can identify challenges, allocate resources more effectively, and create solutions that benefit communities. At the same time, social data can be complex because people's behaviors, cultures, and circumstances cannot always be reduced to simple numbers. That is why it is important to combine technical skills with knowledge from fields like sociology, psychology, economics, and ethics.

Using data about people also comes with significant responsibilities. Privacy is one of the first concerns that comes to mind. People should know how their data is being collected and used, and organizations should protect that information from misuse. Another responsibility is being aware of bias. If a dataset is incomplete or reflects historical inequalities, a model trained on it may produce unfair results. As future data scientists, we need to question where the data comes from, who might be left out, and how our work could affect different groups of people. Accuracy is important, but fairness, transparency, and accountability are just as essential.

Through this course, I hope to better understand how to work with data responsibly while building strong technical skills. I want to learn not only how to collect, analyze, and visualize data but also how to interpret results in ways that are meaningful and ethical. I also hope to gain a better understanding of how data science can support decision-making across different disciplines and contribute to solving real-world problems. By the end of the course, I want to feel more confident using data thoughtfully, recognizing both its potential and its limitations.
